Last night _K_ and the kids joined me at [AAMI Park] for [Iron Maiden]'s [biggest concert in Australia to date][pre-show reel] as part of the [Run for Your Lives] world tour celebrating 50 years. I've lost count of the number of times I've seen Iron Maiden, but as I said back in [September 2005] it's... _a lot_.

[![Run for Your Lives tour poster][RFYL poster]][Iron Maiden]{: .image-left}

## Support: Megadeth

[Megadeth] opened, as [their touring career comes to a close][global farewell tour]. The rain was falling heavily on the open-air venue. [_Peace Sells_][Peace Sells] (but who's dryin'), I joked, just before they started playing that song. I'm not a Megadeth aficionado but I did enjoy the songs I know, including [_Symphony of Destruction_][Symphony of Destruction] and [_Holy Wars... The Punishment Due_][Holy Wars... The Punishment Due].

## Iron Maiden

As they have done for many years, they started by playing [UFO]'s [_Doctor Doctor_][Doctor Doctor] over the public address system.

Then [_The Ides of March_][The Ides of March] played and the [gigantic display], which has been an important part of this tour, took us on a tour of the East End of London and the venues the band played in the days before their first album, before ending up in Paris for the first live song of the night…

[_Murders in the Rue Morgue_][Murders in the Rue Morgue], which I'd not seen them play live since that [Clive Burr] benefit at the Hammersmith Apollo in [September 2005], opened the set and gave the Melbourne audience their introduction to [Simon Dawson] behind the kit, who plays a lot more like Burr than fan favourite [Nicko McBrain], who [retired from touring] a couple of years ago after a whopping 42 years.

Sticking with the 1981 _Killers_ album, we were treated to [_Wrathchild_][Wrathchild] and [_Killers_][Killers] next.

After a brief introduction from [Bruce Dickinson], the band launched into [_Phantom of the Opera_][Phantom of the Opera] from the band's eponymous debut album, making use of the enormo-screen to set the scene inside an old theatre. Classic Maiden.

[_The Number of the Beast_][The Number of the Beast] has never failed to please a crowd and last night was no different. Even the moist Melbourne weather didn't dampen the singing.

A real highlight of the set was the revival of [_Infinite Dreams_][Infinite Dreams], which the band hasn't played live since the 1988 _Seventh Tour of a Seventh Tour_, as captured on [_Maiden England '88_][Maiden England '88], which is a great live album. Being there to see it live was wonderful.

Then it was back to the 1984 _Powerslave_ album for more classics, with the song [_Powerslave_][Powerslave] being played with the album cover on the screen, followed by [_2 Minutes to Midnight_][2 Minutes to Midnight], another song that had the crowd singing along. I didn't realise that [Adrian Smith] had a [custom guitar] for this track!

Then it was time for [_Rime of the Ancient Mariner_][Rime of the Ancient Mariner], complete with a fantastic film aiding Iron Maiden's telling of Coleridge's poem. I am so glad they included it in the set for this tour.

> Then down in falls comes the rain

Indeed.

After that fourteen-minute-ish epic, the punchier and anthemic [_Run to the Hills_][Run to the Hills] (_run for your lives_) had the crowd singing along again. Always fun!

Back to the [_Seventh Son of a Seventh Son_][Seventh Son of a Seventh Son] album for its title track, which like _Infinite Dreams_, I'd never seen them play live and am so happy that I got that experience. It's such an atmospheric piece with a long, tension-building section, which was just awesome to see live.

If I had to name a favourite Iron Maiden song, it would probably be [_The Trooper_][The Trooper], and I think they've played it every time I've seen them live. I'll never tire of it!

[_Hallowed Be Thy Name_][Hallowed Be Thy Name] is another enduring Iron Maiden song that takes me right back to my school days when I first started listening to the band and buying their cassettes. I once smuggled a wink about its Beckett ancestry into my [Desert Island Discs] picks (did you spot it?).

Of course, Iron Maiden closed the main set with [_Iron Maiden_][Iron Maiden (song)]. How could they not? I'm sure we all knew this wasn't quite the end and after some chanting from a sodden Melburnian crowd, Churchill's speech and the sound of Spitfire engines was coming through the speakers.

The encore took off with [_Aces High_][Aces High], accompanied by a great animation on the screens.

Crowd favourite [_Fear of the Dark_][Fear of the Dark] was next. When the studio album came out, I thought this was a boring song, but I've always enjoyed it live, including [Janick Gers]'s harmonic tricks. This was the "newest" song they played all night. From 1992.

Did they save the best for last? They may well have done. [_Wasted Years_][Wasted Years] is another great Iron Maiden singalong. The digital set looked sensational too.

As the lights came up and we all filed out, Monty Python's [_Always Look on the Bright Side of Life_][Always Look on the Bright Side of Life] played over the public address system, as it has done after every Maiden concert I've been to. It's a great way to end the night.

Concert of a lifetime!

⚒️ Up the irons! 🤘🏼
