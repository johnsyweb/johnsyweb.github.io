Last month I joined a book club started by some colleagues at work, and I'm enjoying the motivation to read books to a schedule and discuss them. The first month's book didn't inspire me, but this month's, [Allen Levi]'s _Theo of Golden_, was a real page-turner.

The novel has a strong Christian tone, especially in the second half of the story, but it's the mystery behind the protagonist and the motivation behind his benevolence that kept me engrossed. The characters develop wonderfully, and the narrative had so much to draw me in. Theo has an appreciation of art, not just paintings, but also literature and music. There are scenes where I can almost smell the coffee in _The Chalice_, an establishment I could picture vividly as if it were in my home suburb (and the descriptions of which certainly made me fancy a cup of coffee more than once).

I do most of my reading on public transport, and this story made me grin while reading about Theo's kind acts, his interest in the people around him, and his subtle humour. There were some scenes I wish I'd not read in public. I'll not spoil them for you, but be prepared for a series of emotional responses when you get to the 'cello recital.

The day after I finished the book, I started again at chapter one, and I'll finish it for a second time before book club meets. I can give no stronger endorsement than this.
